RDFox scaling: one named graph per document subsection

15 size points up to 500,000 named graphs / 14,525,277 quads · 10 queries, 150ms blocks · quad-table-sg-fi, quad-table-sg-pi

Dataset: 29.05 quads per graph, 600-char provenance text, fanout 8.6, seed 42.

  • quad-table-sg-pi held the same 14,525,277 quads in 1.2 GB against 1.4 GB for quad-table-sg-fi — 12% less.
  • Memory scaled with exponent 0.83 (R² 0.981) — sub-linear, so cost per quad falls as the corpus grows. Marginal cost settled near 95 bytes/quad.
  • 4 of 10 queries are constant-time — unchanged across a 51,876× increase in data, all under 40.1 µs at full size. That includes the point lookup the whole provenance pattern exists to serve.
  • Biggest divergence between storage types: q03_graphs_mentioning_hub_entity, where quad-table-sg-pi was 1.9× faster (154.0 ms vs 290.3 ms).

How these are timed. RDFox's own Total statement evaluation time is printed to the millisecond and reads 0.000 s for the fastest queries at every corpus size — which is why they cannot be measured that way. Instead each query is repeated with exec N and the whole block is timed from outside the process, then divided by N; the shell's per-statement cost (~30 µs, measured at each size with a trivial control query) is subtracted. N is chosen per query per size to target a fixed amount of work, so a 14 µs lookup is averaged over thousands of executions and a 290 ms scan over a few. This matters for the conclusions, not just the charts: under millisecond timing the scan queries appeared to scale linearly (1.07–1.31), because the small-size readings were pinned at the resolution floor. Measured properly they are sub-linear (0.65–0.95).
